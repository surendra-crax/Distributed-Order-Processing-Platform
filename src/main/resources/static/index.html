🔥 Why Concurrency Testing Matters (The Black Friday Test)
High-Throughput Concurrency
⚠️ The Real-World Problem
When 20 threads read a database row simultaneously (`stock = 10`), all 20 threads think stock is available. All 20 succeed, resulting in -10 units (lost revenue).
💡 How Our Engine Solves It
PostgreSQL conditionally decrements in an atomic operation. Even with 50 threads firing at the exact same microsecond, zero overselling is guaranteed.

📬 Why The Transactional Outbox Pattern Eliminates "Ghost Orders"
Reliable Messaging
⚠️ The Real-World Problem
If the database commits an order, but sending to Kafka fails, the customer's money is taken, but the warehouse never packs the order. The package is lost forever.
💡 How Our Engine Solves It
We write both the orders row and outbox_events row in the exact same ACID transaction. A dedicated scheduler relays to Kafka with retries.

💡 What Problem Does This Platform Solve in the Real World?

In small college projects or tutorial CRUD apps, a purchase simply does db.save(order) and stock = stock - 1. That works when 1 user is on the site.

At enterprise scale (Amazon, Uber, Flipkart, DoorDash), this simple code causes catastrophic business failures:

1. Flash-Sale Overselling Disaster
When 50,000 customers buy 500 iPhones at midnight, in-memory checks fail. 5,000 people are charged, but only 500 phones exist. Amazon faces refunds and lawsuits.
Our Solution: Atomic conditional database updates (WHERE available >= qty) guarantee zero overselling.
2. The Wi-Fi Drop Double-Charge Bug
If a customer's phone Wi-Fi drops while paying and they tap "Pay" twice, naive apps charge their credit card twice and deliver duplicate pizzas.
Our Solution: Idempotency keys + SHA-256 payload hashing guarantee exactly-one charge.
3. The Dual-Write Ghost Order Bug
Writing to PostgreSQL succeeded, but sending to Kafka failed. The customer's money is taken, but the warehouse never packs the order.
Our Solution: Transactional Outbox commits the order and event in the same ACID transaction.
